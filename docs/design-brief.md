# Design brief

Design step 1 (CLAUDE.md §5.2). Confirms what every later design decision gets checked against.

## Audiences (two doors, one body of work)

1. **Hire me** — GRC/compliance/IT audit hiring managers (primary), M365/IT admin/IAM hiring managers (secondary). They want: controls, evidence, remediation, hands-on M365/Windows work, clear writing.
2. **Work with me** — small businesses, authors, local organisations, nonprofits. They want: a real client site that works, what Michael can do for them, how to start a conversation.

Both doors open onto the same case studies, distinguished only by an audience tag (`grc`, `m365`, `iam`, `endpoint`, `web`, `automation`). The home page leads with the hire-me position; the client door gets its own clearly labelled band, never mixed into the job pitch. Pricing and "how we'd work together" live only on `/work-with-me/`.

## Position line

> **IT and GRC, built hands-on.** Six years in Amazon operations taught me that a control only works when people follow it and you can prove it. Now I build the systems and the evidence, for employers and for small businesses.

This is the home page headline/subline candidate (Michael may rewrite it into his own voice — see CLAUDE.md §10's note that this is a draft to edit, not final copy). Every page should support it; nothing should contradict it.

## The three words

**Clear, credible, quiet.** Test every design choice against these:

- **Clear** — a visitor can scan any page in 10 seconds and know what it proves. No decoration that doesn't aid scanning.
- **Credible** — structure does the persuading: status badges, verification lines, "what went wrong" sections. No skill bars, no percentages, no "expert" labels (Michael is entry-level and the site says so with confidence, not apology).
- **Quiet** — one accent colour, generous white space, calm like a well-made audit report. Distinctive through structure, not styling.

If an element doesn't make a page clearer or more credible, it goes, even if it's quiet. If it's loud, it goes regardless of the other two.

## What the site must never do

(Full list in CLAUDE.md §1 "What the site must not do" — the load-bearing ones for design specifically:)

- No skill bars, percentages, or "expert"/"mastery" labels.
- No fake testimonials, no stock client logos, no Amazon/WGU logos as images.
- Status badges always carry a word, never colour alone (accessibility + honesty — a colour-blind reader or a screen reader user must get the same information).
- Every case study shows its verification line even when it's not flattering ("built against test fixtures," not yet "verified live").
