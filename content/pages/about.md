# A control only works if people follow it, and you can prove it

I learned that on a warehouse floor before I ever learned it from a framework.

## Credentials

- **Based in:** Orlando, FL, or remote
- **Certifications:** CompTIA Security+, Network+ and A+ (2025); ITIL 4 Foundation in IT Service Management (2026)
- **Degree:** B.S. Cybersecurity and Information Assurance, Western Governors University — in progress, expected January 2028
- **Also:** Full Stack Web Development Certificate, University of Central Florida
- **Looking for:** Entry-level GRC and compliance, IT audit, Microsoft 365 administration, or identity and access

## How I got here

I started at Amazon in 2020 as an associate, and from 2021 I was a Stow Process Assistant, leading daily start-ups for 60–80 associates and coaching on quality. When I moved to quality at a new site launch, our weekly SOX audit compliance sat around 63–70%. I set up structured tracking and trained associates on every shift, and within a month we were at 99% and stayed there.

That's when I learned that controls are a people problem as much as a technical one, and that I like the work of making them stick. A control that nobody follows isn't a control, and one you can't evidence may as well not exist.

## What I build

I learn by building, and then by auditing what I built. Three examples, each with its own case study:

- [**Workspace**](/work/workspace/) — a SharePoint-shaped intranet that runs on three different backends, with 454 automated checks and a stored-XSS flaw I found and fixed across 60 insertion points.
- [**A cyber home lab**](/work/cyber-homelab/) — Entra ID and SharePoint governance in a live Microsoft 365 tenant, then reviewed against itself. That review is how I found the audit log had never been switched on.
- [**A GRC program for a business that doesn't exist**](/work/grc-security-program/) — Burn and Churn Coffee, a fictional card-accepting small business, taken from asset inventory through a 15-control matrix to a POA&M.

**WinSysConfig** is the one without a write-up yet: a Windows settings tool that journals every change it makes, with no network listener at all. It gets its own case study once its writes and journal are pushed.

## The kind of team I'm looking for

I'd especially value a team that mentors. I'm early enough in this that being taught properly is worth more to me than a title, and I'd rather be somewhere that expects questions than somewhere that assumes I have none.
